# Marketing Claims Reference — Quantified vs. Estimated

**Purpose:** internal reference to keep language consistent and defensible across the website, quotes, and sales conversations. Use the "Confidence" rating to decide how firm a claim can be — high confidence = safe to state as fact/number; low confidence = frame as mechanism or practitioner consensus, not a percentage.

---

## Water & Earthworks

### Swales
- **Runoff reduction:** normal rainfall events can see 30-90%+ runoff volume reduction on appropriately sloped, well-sited swales; effectiveness drops sharply for extreme storm events (single-digit % reduction in 50-year-storm conditions)
- **Groundwater infiltration increase:** vegetated/mulched treatments have shown infiltration gains in the 20-60% range in controlled comparisons
- **Design constraint:** most effective on slopes of 5% or less (some designs extend to ~15% with reinforcement)
- **Confidence: Moderate** — real field/hydrology data exists, but figures are site-specific (soil, slope, watershed size, storm intensity). Safe to say "substantially reduces runoff and increases infiltration for normal rainfall" — avoid a single pinned percentage without a site-specific study.

### Keyline plowing / subsoiling
- Commonly claimed to significantly improve infiltration by breaking compaction layers
- **Confidence: Low-moderate** — mostly practitioner-reported, limited independent controlled trials. Frame as "improves infiltration by decompacting subsoil," not a number.

### Hugelkultur
- **Water retention:** wood acts as a sponge, buffering moisture over multiple years — reasonably well-supported mechanism
- **Nutrient release:** decomposing wood releases nutrients over a multi-year timeline as it breaks down (commonly cited up to ~20 years for full hardwood decomposition), but **no peer-reviewed studies exist quantifying nutrient output**, and the limited available research found no clear plant nitrogen deficiency but couldn't isolate hugelkultur's specific contribution from other variables (e.g., mulch layer composition)
- **Caveat:** fresh wood ties up nitrogen for roughly the first season before nutrient release accelerates — don't claim immediate fertility benefit
- **Confidence: Low on nutrients, moderate on water retention.** Sell hugelkultur primarily as a water-retention/moisture-buffering feature. Avoid specific NPK or nutrient-release numbers — no credible source supports them.

### Check dams / gully plugs
- Reduce channel erosion and trap sediment; magnitude entirely site- and design-dependent
- **Confidence: Low** — no general-purpose percentage exists without a specific site study.

### Ponds / rainwater catchment earthworks
- Directly calculable per site: catchment area × rainfall × runoff coefficient = harvestable volume
- **Confidence: High** — this is standard hydrology math, not a claim. Always quote actual numbers per property rather than a generic figure.

## Planting Systems

### Nitrogen-fixing pioneer trees
- Symbiotic rhizobia fix atmospheric nitrogen into soil over the plant's life, released as leaves/roots die back
- **Confidence: Moderate** — well-established soil science mechanism, but rate varies hugely by species, climate, and soil. Name the mechanism, avoid a universal number.

### Windbreaks / shelterbelts
- Reduce wind speed and evapotranspiration in a zone extending roughly 10x the windbreak's height downwind
- **Confidence: Moderate-high** — established in agricultural shelterbelt design literature.

## Off-Grid Infrastructure

### Well
- Yield (gallons/minute) is measured directly during drilling — a hard, site-specific number
- **Confidence: High** — always cite the client's actual drilled yield, never a generic estimate.

### Solar + battery
- Sizing is directly calculable from daily load (kWh/day) ÷ panel output ÷ battery capacity
- **Confidence: High** — standard electrical engineering math, quote per-property.

## Aquaculture (Pond Fish / Trout)

- **Unfed pond yield:** ~50-100 lbs trout per surface acre on natural food alone
- **Fed pond yield:** 1,000+ lbs per acre possible with regular pelleted feed
- **Stocking density (volume basis):** ~1-2 lbs trout per cubic foot of water
- **Feed conversion ratio:** ~1.2-1.5 lbs feed per lb of fish gained under typical small-scale conditions (as low as 0.8:1 with optimized commercial feed)
- **Overwintering depth:** 4-6 ft minimum recommended depth for cold-hardy species in an Edmonton-climate pond, ideally below the local frost line (~5-8 ft in the Edmonton area) or paired with active aeration/de-icing
- **Confidence: Moderate-high** — figures come from established small-scale aquaculture sources, but should be presented as ranges, not guarantees, since local water quality/management heavily affects real results.

## Security

### Cameras
- **Best-quality finding:** across a 161-study meta-analysis, CCTV produced an average ~13% crime reduction overall — but the effect is concentrated almost entirely in **actively monitored** systems (~15%, rising to ~34% when layered with other measures like lighting/signage). Passive, record-only cameras showed **no statistically significant deterrent effect**.
- **Offender self-report data:** ~60% of surveyed convicted burglars said a visible security system (including cameras) influenced them to avoid a property; ~83% said they'd check for an alarm/system before attempting entry.
- **Displacement caveat:** roughly 15-20% of deterred crime simply shifts to a neighboring unprotected property rather than being eliminated — worth mentioning to clients for honest framing.
- **Confidence: Moderate-high on the meta-analysis figures** (real academic source). **Low confidence on vendor-marketing numbers** like "300% safer" or "67% reduction in break-ins" — these appear across many vendor sites without a traceable rigorous source; avoid repeating them.
- **Key sales point:** monitoring capability (even basic app alerts) is what drives the real effect — don't sell passive-only camera installs as having strong deterrent value.

### Fencing
- **No rigorous quantitative studies exist** — available claims are almost entirely vendor marketing, not criminology research.
- **Mechanism (defensible without a number):** fencing works by increasing effort and time-on-target during a break-in attempt, not by adding surveillance/identification risk the way cameras do. This raises exposure time and disrupts a burglar's preference for fast, low-risk entry.
- **Design specifics that matter more than "having a fence":**
  - Height: 6+ ft commonly cited threshold for meaningfully harder climbing
  - Material: solid/reinforced barriers resist cutting better than standard chain-link
  - Sightlines: open-view fencing (mesh, wrought iron) preserves visibility for neighbors/cameras — fully opaque fencing can backfire by hiding an intruder once inside
  - Anti-climb features (spikes, toppers, smooth surfaces) target the effort/time mechanism directly
- **Confidence: Low on any percentage. Moderate on the mechanism itself.** Sell fencing on "increases time, effort, and exposure for an intruder" language — never attach an unsourced percentage.

### Combined (fencing + cameras + lighting)
- Layered security measures showed roughly double the deterrent effect (~34%) compared to monitored cameras alone (~15%) in the meta-analysis referenced above.
- **Confidence: Moderate** — legitimate, sourced rationale for selling bundled packages over single-item installs.

## General Messaging Guidance

1. **High-confidence numbers** (well yield, solar sizing, pond catchment volume, camera monitoring meta-analysis) → safe to quote directly and specifically.
2. **Moderate-confidence mechanisms** (swales, N-fixers, windbreaks, fencing, aquaculture yields) → describe the mechanism and give a *range*, not a single hard percentage; frame as "typically" or "can."
3. **Low-confidence claims** (hugelkultur nutrient output, keyline infiltration %, check dam effectiveness, most vendor security stats) → avoid numbers entirely; sell on mechanism and practitioner consensus, and be ready to say "this is well-observed anecdotally but not rigorously measured" if a client or competitor pushes back.
4. Never repeat unsourced vendor statistics (e.g., "300% safer," "67% reduction") even though they're common across competitor sites — they don't hold up if challenged and undermine credibility on the numbers you *can* defend.
