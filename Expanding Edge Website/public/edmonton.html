Why Capital Region Land Needs Local Permaculture

Edmonton’s fringe and rural belt is full of productive potential — black and grey-wooded soils, aspen parkland microclimates, and acreages that want food, privacy, and lower inputs. It also brings clay pans, spring melt, late frost, and wind. Generic landscaping rarely solves that. Expanding Edge designs and installs systems that fit Capital Region realities: water on contour, cold-hardy plantings, zone planning for real Alberta winters, and phased builds acreage owners can actually fund.

Climate & Site Notes for the Capital Region

Growing season

Short but intense. We prioritize perennial structure, season extension, and microclimates (south walls, windbreaks, thermal mass).

Soils & water

Many sites mix loam with heavy clay. Contour work and organic matter build infiltration; wet corners become assets, not liabilities.

Wind & exposure

Open parkland needs shelterbelts and edge design. Placement of home, garden, and livestock follows energy flows, not only aesthetics.
